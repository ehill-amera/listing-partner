# The workflow, end to end

What a photographer or property manager actually does with Listing Partner, from arriving at a vacant unit to a listing ready to post. No flash, no Lightroom.

## 1. Shoot (on site)

Same for every camera.
- Tripod, level, at about chest height, with the lens at its widest that does not distort.
- Fixed settings for each room: aperture, ISO (as low as the camera allows), white balance (a fixed setting, not Auto), focus locked.
- Bracket every shot. The darkest frame holds the view out the window. The brightest frame shows clean detail in the darkest corner. Fill in at about 1 stop apart, usually 5 to 9 frames on a bright day.
- A timer or remote, so touching the camera never moves it.
- Shoot every room plus the front exterior. The coverage check will say if something was missed.

**DSLR or mirrorless:** raw (or raw plus JPEG), manual exposure, step the shutter between frames, or use the camera's bracketing if it covers the range.

**iPhone:** a phone mount on any steady tripod. ProCamera with Exposure Bracketing in RAW (verified on ProCamera's own blog, needs its paid upgrade), 1x main lens, focus and exposure locked. The phone's 3-frame bracketing covers less range than a camera, so very bright windows may still clip. Phone files are untested in the pipeline until a real phone set is run.

## 2. Drop (back at the desk, 1 minute)

Copy the card or phone files into the drop folder. One folder per unit. Nothing else.

## 3. The agent works (no one watching)

Merge brackets, fix the lens and verticals, find and pull every window (all windows in a photo in one pass, however many there are), balance exposure, white balance, denoise, cull and order, label rooms, check coverage, pull the unit facts, write the description and captions, check fair housing wording, list maintenance issues, tidy clutter and replace gray exterior skies inside saved masks only (each labeled), export at listing size, save a 16-bit master. It checks its own work against its definition of done, retries what fails, and stops with a plain list of anything it cannot fix.

## 4. Review (the person, a few minutes)

Open one page. Look at the photos beside their originals, read the description, read the reshoot list and the maintenance list. Approve, or paint over anything the tidy missed (stretch goal).

## 5. Post (the person)

Copy the photos and description into wherever the listing goes (for Bokani, AppFolio, which syndicates rentals). The agent does not post anywhere by itself in this version.

## Not in this version (roadmap, after Nov 4)

- Posting directly to listing platforms. Each platform has its own access rules, and MLS input usually requires a licensed member and approved tools.
- Pulling and cross-checking property facts from public records (county tax and assessor data) against the manager's own records.
- Twilight shots, drone, video tours, virtual staging.
